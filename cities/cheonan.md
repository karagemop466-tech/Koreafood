# Cheonan Food Guide — Verified Entries Only

**Verification status:** every row below was checked against an official source (VisitKorea — the Korea Tourism Organization portal, operated as *대한민국 구석구석*) in August 2026. Entries whose opening hours could not be confirmed from an official source have been removed from this guide entirely, per the verification policy.

**No prices are listed.** Earlier drafts carried "Est. Price Range" figures with no traceable source.

**Michelin note:** the MICHELIN Guide's Korean selection covers **Seoul and Busan only**. There is no MICHELIN Guide coverage of Cheonan, so any Michelin claim about a Cheonan restaurant is false by definition.

**What Cheonan is for:** two things, and they are worth the stop. **Byeongcheon (Aunae) sundae** — the national reference point for sundae-gukbap — and **hodu-gwaja**, the walnut cake invented here in 1934. Byeongcheon is a 40-minute bus ride east of Cheonan Station, not a downtown neighbourhood; budget half a day.

## Byeongcheon Sundae (병천순대) — the reason to come

Byeongcheon sundae uses the **small intestine** rather than the large, so it is markedly less funky than sundae elsewhere, and it is stuffed with vegetables and seonji (blood curd) instead of the usual glass noodles. The broth is pork-bone, pale but not heavy. The trade started when a meat-processing plant opened nearby in the 1950s and locals took home the offcuts; it became a fixed daily business, rather than a market-day-only one, from **1968**.

## Unverified leads — listed, not recommended

These names circulate in Cheonan food coverage but have **no official portal listing** with hours, so they are not part of the verified guide. Do not treat them as researched:

- **Park Sun-ja Aunae Sundae** (박순자아우내순대) — Byeongcheon
- **Wonjo Hakhwa Halmeoni Hodo-gwaja, Byeongcheon branch** (원조학화할머니호도과자 병천점) — Aunae-sundae-gil, ☎ 041-569-6677. Cited in a VisitKorea travel *article* but has no restaurant listing of its own. The Hakhwa lineage traces to Jo Gwi-geum and Sim Bok-sun, who invented hodu-gwaja in Cheonan in 1934; the family's original shop is in Guseong-dong.
